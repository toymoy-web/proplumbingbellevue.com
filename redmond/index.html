Coverage & Local Notes

Redmond's Split Housing Stock, By ZIP

Redmond was founded in 1871 and incorporated in 1912, which makes its downtown core, near the Sammamish River and Marymoor Park, one of the older residential areas in this part of the Eastside. Homes here in the 98052 ZIP often predate the tech boom that reshaped the city, and many still carry original galvanized supply lines from that earlier era.

Out in 98073, Redmond Ridge tells a different story: it's a planned community built mostly in the 2000s, with PEX and copper plumbing throughout and none of the corrosion issues we see closer to downtown. Overlake, near the Microsoft campus, sits somewhere in between, with a mix of older ramblers and newer dense condo development.

Redmond is also one of the only cities in this service area that supplies part of its own water rather than buying entirely through Cascade Water Alliance, drawing roughly 40 percent from the city's own aquifer and the rest via Seattle's Tolt River pipeline.

Redmond has been nicknamed the Bicycle Capital of the Northwest, and the city's dense network of trails along the Sammamish River and through Marymoor Park shapes how neighborhoods connect. It doesn't change how we approach a plumbing call, though: we still diagnose based on the home's actual age and system, not just which part of the city it's in. A same-day drain call in Overlake gets the same urgency as one downtown.

What Redmond Homeowners Call About Most

Repiping In The Older Core, Tankless Service In The New

The calls we get in Redmond split almost exactly along the city's housing-era line. Downtown and older Overlake homes call about pressure that's dropped over the years, usually pointing to galvanized pipe finally reaching the end of its service life. Redmond Ridge and newer condo calls are more often about a tankless system due for its first descaling, or a fixture in a dense multi-unit building. Knowing which situation applies before we arrive helps us bring the right parts and equipment on the first visit rather than a second trip. Every repair, in either part of the city, is completed to Uniform Plumbing Code standards by a Washington L&I-licensed plumber.

If You Have A Burst Pipe Or Active Leak In Redmond
