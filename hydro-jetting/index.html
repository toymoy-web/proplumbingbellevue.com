Hydro Jetting in Bellevue, WA

High-pressure water line clearing for grease buildup and root intrusion a standard cable snake can't fully clear.

Best ForRecurring Clogs

Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water, not a mechanical cable, to clear a line completely rather than just punching a path through a blockage. It's the right tool for grease buildup that's coated the inside of a pipe over years, or for root intrusion that a snake can knock back but not remove.

We camera-inspect the line before jetting whenever a clog is recurring, both to confirm jetting is the right fix and to check the pipe's condition first. Jetting is safe for sound pipe but can be too aggressive for a line that's already cracked or significantly deteriorated, so we check before we jet, not after. That order matters more than it might seem.

We also use hydro jetting preventively for properties with a known history of root intrusion, scheduling a jetting pass on a set interval rather than waiting for the next clog. For a property that's had two or more root-related clogs in past years, this often works out cheaper over time than repeated emergency snaking calls.

For commercial kitchens specifically, hydro jetting is often the right first call rather than a last resort. Grease buildup in a commercial line accumulates faster and more consistently than in a typical residential system, and a standard snake often isn't enough to keep a busy kitchen's lines genuinely clear between visits. We treat commercial jetting as routine maintenance for kitchens on a schedule, not solely as an emergency response.

When A Line Keeps Clogging After Snaking

If a drain has been snaked more than once and the clog keeps coming back within a few weeks, that's usually a sign the snake is punching a hole through the blockage rather than actually clearing the line. Grease buildup along the pipe wall and root intrusion both cause this pattern, and both respond to hydro jetting in a way that repeated snaking doesn't.

What Affects The Price

Hydro jetting is priced by the line's total length and how much buildup or root intrusion is actually present, confirmed by the camera inspection before any work formally starts.

Line length

A longer main sewer run takes more time to fully jet than a short branch line.

Severity of buildup

Heavy grease buildup or significant root intrusion takes longer to clear than a lighter blockage.

Access points

An existing cleanout makes access straightforward; if one isn't present, adding one changes the scope.
