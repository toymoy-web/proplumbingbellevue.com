ServiceScheduled & As-Needed

A grease trap that isn't cleaned on schedule backs up slowly at first, a floor drain that's slower than usual, then all at once when it finally can't hold any more. We service traps on a routine schedule for food service clients and handle emergency cleanouts when a trap has already backed up.

King County health code requires commercial kitchens to maintain functioning grease interceptors, and inspectors do check maintenance records. We can set up a routine schedule that keeps a property compliant, not just functional, with records kept on file for the next inspection, referencing the same Uniform Plumbing Code interceptor-sizing standards King County enforces.

We set most commercial kitchens up on a recurring schedule rather than waiting for a backup. The cost of routine service is consistently lower than an emergency cleanout plus the downtime of a kitchen that can't run its dish pit or prep sinks during service hours.

For newer downtown Bellevue restaurant spaces built into mixed-use developments, trap access can be tighter than in a standalone building, and we account for that when scheduling, since some interceptors are located in spaces that need coordination with building management to reach.

We also handle grease trap sizing consultations for new restaurant builds and remodels, since an undersized interceptor is one of the more common reasons a new kitchen ends up on a much more frequent cleaning schedule than it should be. Getting the size right at the start, based on King County's requirements for your specific type of food service establishment, generally saves money over the life of the equipment compared to correcting an undersized system after the fact. We can review plans or an existing setup and tell you honestly whether the current interceptor actually fits your kitchen's real volume.
