The Neighborhood

Plumbing In Lakemont And Cougar Mountain

Cougar Mountain/Lakemont is one of the City of Bellevue's neighborhood areas, covering roughly four square miles in the southeast. The city's profile describes views of Lake Sammamish and the Cascades to the east and of downtown Bellevue, Lake Washington and Seattle to the west. It counts 11,416 residents in 3,990 households, about 97% occupied, in housing that is primarily single-family.

The profile says this area has seen more growth than any other part of Bellevue outside Downtown over the past two decades. Many developments have community-owned greenbelts and strict covenants to protect views. Local landmarks include Lakemont Village Shopping Center, Cougar Ridge Elementary School and 25 parks, among them Coal Creek Park, Lewis Creek Park, Saddleback Park and Lakemont Community Park.

A trail system connects homes to parks, Cougar Mountain Regional Wildland Park and Lakemont Village. These figures are from the city's profile, published before the 2025 and 2026 reports on other neighborhoods, so recent growth may have changed them.

Good To Know

Hillside Homes And Water Pressure

As general plumbing information, water pressure tends to be higher at lower elevations and lower at higher ones, and many homes use a pressure-reducing valve to keep pressure in a safe range. Banging pipes, a dripping relief valve or fixtures that spit can all be signs that pressure is worth testing. This is general guidance, not a finding about any specific street.

Many neighborhoods here have homeowners associations with architectural rules. If you are replacing an exterior vent or changing equipment placement, check your HOA requirements before the work is scheduled.

Water heater placement matters in newer homes too. Units in garages or utility closets need proper clearance, venting and, where required, a drain pan, and a tank benefits from an annual flush. Ask for a simple maintenance routine whenever a plumber is already on site.

Before You Call

If A Tankless Water Heater Shows An Error

  • Write down the error code shown on the display.
  • Check that gas or power is on and the shutoff valves to the unit are open.
  • Avoid repeatedly resetting the unit, and note whether the problem happens with hot water only.
  • Call and share the code, brand and model so the plumber arrives prepared.
