Coverage & Local Notes

Plumbing In Renton, From The Landing To The Highlands

Renton is the largest city we cover beyond Bellevue, with about 106,800 residents at the 2020 census. It spreads across the southeast shore of Lake Washington at the mouth of the Cedar River. Our Bellevue base is roughly 11 miles to the north by I-405, about a 20 to 25 minute drive in normal traffic.

Renton was settled in the 1860s and incorporated on September 6, 1901, when coal mining and timber processing drove the economy. Boeing changed everything. The company built its Renton plant during World War II to produce the B-29, and the city grew from 4,488 people in 1940 to 16,039 in 1950. Many homes built in that boom are now past 70 years old.

The city is a patchwork of distinct areas. Downtown and The Landing sit near the lake and the Boeing factory, and Kennydale runs along the Lake Washington shoreline to the north. The Renton Highlands, Benson Hill, Cascade, and Fairwood cover the hills and plateaus to the east and south. Local real estate prices these micro-markets differently, and the plumbing differs along the same lines.

Older core neighborhoods tend to have aging supply lines and sewer laterals. Plateau subdivisions built later more often have newer drain pipe and PEX supply lines. A single home can be a mix after past remodels. We identify what is actually in the walls before recommending a repair, and we explain the options plainly. Work follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted in Washington, and permits go through the City of Renton when a job requires one.

Renton runs its own city water utility, separate from the Cascade Water Alliance that serves Bellevue. Pressure and billing questions go to the City of Renton. Problems inside your home, such as a leak, a failed water heater, or a slow drain, are ours to fix.

Gene Coulon Memorial Beach Park on the lake, the Cedar River Trail, and the open ground around the municipal airport show how much of Renton sits low and close to water. Low ground near the lake and the river mouth is where sump pump and drainage questions come up most often.
