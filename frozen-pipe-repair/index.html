Bellevue doesn't see the sustained hard freezes that make frozen pipes a routine winter problem the way colder climates do. A cold snap with temperatures well below freezing for several days still puts exposed or poorly insulated pipes at real risk, especially in crawlspaces, garages, and exterior walls not built with extreme cold in mind.

If a pipe is frozen but hasn't cracked yet, we thaw it carefully to avoid the pressure buildup that causes a break. If it's already cracked, and freeze breaks often aren't visible until the ice thaws and water starts flowing, we shut off the water and repair the pipe, then check for damage the water may have already caused. Insulation and freeze protection for exposed pipe follow standard building code minimums for this climate zone.

The pipes most at risk in a Bellevue cold snap are the ones that weren't really designed for sustained freezing temperatures in the first place: exterior hose bibs, supply lines in an unheated garage, and crawlspace runs in older homes with minimal insulation. We can point out which specific lines in your home fall into that category and recommend insulation before the next cold snap, not just after a pipe has already frozen once.

For vacation homes or properties left unoccupied during a cold snap, a frozen pipe often isn't discovered until well after it's thawed and already caused significant water damage. There are more homes like this in the Bellevue area than people expect, given how many households travel for extended periods. We can set up a periodic check during forecasted hard freezes, which catches a frozen line before it becomes a much larger repair and cleanup.

Emergency Response

Emergency Frozen Pipe Response Before It Bursts

A frozen pipe is an emergency before it ever bursts. Ice blocks the flow, pressure builds behind the blockage, and the pipe can split as it thaws, so flooding often starts only after temperatures climb.

If a faucet runs a trickle or nothing at all in freezing weather, open that faucet, keep the heat on, and call our emergency line. Never use an open flame on a pipe. If you can see a split, shut off the main valve right away.

Bellevue Utilities advises insulating pipes in exposed or unheated areas, disconnecting and draining hoses, and letting faucets on exposed pipes drip during a freeze. Those steps lower the risk, and we handle the repair when they are not enough.

Before We Arrive

If You Find A Frozen Pipe

  • Shut off the water supply to that fixture or the main if the pipe has already cracked.
  • Don't use an open flame to thaw a pipe. A hair dryer or heat lamp on an exposed, accessible section is safer.
  • Leave a nearby faucet slightly open while thawing so melting water has somewhere to go and relieves pressure.
  • Call us if you can't reach the frozen section safely, or if you're not sure whether it's already cracked.
