Electronic leak tracing for slab leaks and hidden pipe damage, so we find the source before opening anything up.

Leak detection is the single most common call we get, and for good reason: a hidden leak rarely announces itself clearly. A warm spot on the floor, a slightly higher water bill, a faint sound of running water when nothing's turned on, these are all real signs, but none of them point to an exact location on their own.

We use acoustic and electronic leak detection equipment to trace the source without cutting into drywall, flooring, or concrete first. For a slab leak specifically, that means finding the exact point under the foundation before any demolition happens, which saves real money compared to opening up a large section of slab to search.

We also handle leak detection for irrigation systems, which fail differently than household plumbing and often show up first as an unexplained wet patch in the yard rather than an indoor sign. Since irrigation lines are typically shallower and more accessible than a home's main supply lines, detection and repair on these is usually faster and less invasive.

We also perform leak detection as part of a pre-purchase inspection for homebuyers who want confidence in a property's plumbing before closing, since a hidden leak is exactly the kind of issue a standard visual home inspection can miss entirely. For an older Bellevue home with galvanized supply lines, this extra step can surface a problem while there's still room to negotiate, rather than discovering it as a new owner a few months after moving in.

When You Know There's A Leak But Can't See One

The most common call we get on this is a water bill that's climbed without an obvious reason, or a water meter that keeps running even with every fixture in the house off. Neither tells you where the leak is, only that one exists. That's exactly what leak detection equipment is for: finding the source without guessing or opening up walls and flooring on a hunch.

Local Neighborhoods

Finding Hidden Leaks In Older Homes And Towers

Homes in Lake Hills and Crossroads from the 1950s and 1960s can have slab floors or low crawlspaces, so a leak can hide for months. The usual clue is a water bill that climbs with no visible cause, or a warm spot on a floor. In Downtown and BelRed buildings, a leak can travel along a ceiling before it shows, so the source may be several units away from the stain.

Pricing

What Affects The Price

Leak detection is priced by how extensive the search needs to be, not by guesswork, and the number is separate from whatever repair follows once the source is found.

Type of leak

A slab leak typically takes longer to trace than a leak in an accessible wall or crawlspace.

System size

A larger home with more plumbing runs takes longer to isolate the affected section.

Access

Finished spaces without easy access to plumbing take more time than an open crawlspace or basement.

Before We Arrive

Signs Worth A Leak Detection Call

  • A water bill that's risen without a clear reason, or a change in usage habits.
  • A water meter that keeps moving even with every fixture in the house off.
  • A warm or damp spot on the floor, especially over a slab foundation.
  • A faint sound of running water when nothing is turned on.
