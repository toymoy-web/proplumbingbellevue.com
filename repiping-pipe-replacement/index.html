ScopeAfter Inspection

A whole-home repipe is a bigger decision than most plumbing calls, and we treat it that way: we inspect the existing system, identify which sections are actually failing versus which are still sound, and scope a plan before quoting a number.

Galvanized steel pipe was standard in homes built through the 1960s and into the 70s, and it has a real service life. Internal corrosion narrows the pipe over decades until pressure drops and, eventually, a joint fails outright. Polybutylene pipe, used in some construction from the late 1970s through the 1990s, has its own known failure pattern and is a repipe candidate on its own merits, regardless of visible symptoms yet.

We also offer partial repiping for homeowners who aren't ready for a full whole-home project but have one clearly failing section, a bathroom with persistent low pressure, for example, while the rest of the system is still sound. It's a reasonable middle step, though we'll be upfront if a partial repipe is likely to just delay a fuller project rather than actually solve the underlying issue.

We phase larger repipe projects when a homeowner needs to manage cash flow rather than paying for a full-home project at once, completing the most critical sections first, usually wherever active symptoms already exist, and scheduling the remaining sections over subsequent visits. It's a reasonable way to spread out a genuinely large expense, though we'll walk through the trade-offs honestly, since phasing does mean living with a mixed old-and-new system for longer during the transition.

When Low Water Pressure Actually Means The Pipes Are Failing

Homeowners often call about water pressure that's dropped over months or years, or a home where pressure is fine at one fixture and weak at another on the same floor. That gradual pattern, rather than a sudden change, is a common sign of galvanized pipe corroding from the inside and narrowing the passage rather than a single blockage. It's worth a real inspection before assuming a fixture or the city supply is the cause.

Local Neighborhoods

Repiping Homes Of The Lake Hills And Crossroads Era

Lake Hills homes began going up in 1955, the Crossroads shopping center opened in 1962, and Newport Hills was built largely in the late 1950s and 1960s. Homes from that era were commonly plumbed with galvanized steel, and many have been partly repiped over the years. A partial repipe can leave old and new pipe side by side, so we map which sections remain before pricing a full replacement.

Pricing

What Affects The Price

Repiping is priced after inspection, since it depends heavily on the home's layout, how many fixtures are involved, and how accessible the existing pipe runs are.

Home size and layout

More fixtures and floors means more pipe runs and more access points.

Pipe material chosen

PEX is typically faster to install than copper, which affects labor cost.

Wall and floor access

A single-story home with a crawlspace is usually faster to repipe than a multi-story home requiring wall access on every floor.

Permits

A whole-home repipe typically requires a permit through King County or the City of Bellevue, which we handle as part of the scope.

Galvanized Pipe

Replacing Galvanized Supply Pipe

Galvanized steel corrodes from the inside, so pressure drops and rusty water show up long before the pipe leaks. Callers often ask about it after a plumber finds corrosion during another repair.

We scope the whole supply system and explain what we find. If only part of the house is affected, a phased repipe or a targeted replacement may make sense, and we say so plainly.

Whole House Or Main Line

Whole-House Repipe Or Main Line Only

Some homeowners ask for the whole house to be done rather than only the main line. That is a reasonable choice when the pipe on both sides of the meter is the same age and material.

We quote both options so the difference is clear, and a repipe can also be done in stages around remodel work.
