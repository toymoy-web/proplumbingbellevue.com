Kirkland vs Bellevue Water Heater Permits: What Differs

Two neighboring cities with similar rules and a few real differences, from the cities' own permit pages.

By the Bellevue Pro Plumbing team · September 2026 · Updated October 2026

Bellevue and Kirkland sit right next to each other and have similar Eastside housing stock. Both require permits to replace a water heater, and the paperwork looks alike. The differences are in the details, and they are worth understanding before you schedule anything.

How Each City Handles Gas And Electric Units

Kirkland's permit page says a gas water heater replacement needs a mechanical permit, plus a plumbing permit if the water piping is rearranged. An electric replacement needs a plumbing permit. Kirkland adds a mechanical permit if old gas lines are removed or capped, and an electrical permit if you replace a gas heater with an electric one. A building permit applies if walls, floors or ceilings must be opened.

Bellevue treats the two fuel types the same way in principle. Its plumbing permit page lists replacing an electric water heater, and says replacing a gas water heater requires a mechanical permit. A gas water heater involves combustion, venting and gas supply, which is mechanical code territory, while the water connections are plumbing code territory.

Water Heater Permits In Bellevue

Bellevue's permit page says a permit is required for replacing or moving an existing water heater. Replacing an electric water heater needs a plumbing permit, and replacing a gas water heater needs a mechanical permit. Permits that do not require plan review can be issued the same day, and the permits still require inspections.

Bellevue also has a clear emergency rule. The city says it does not require a permit before an emergency repair, but you must obtain the permit on the first city work day after the work started. The city gives replacing a broken water heater as a common example.

Kirkland's page says single-family water heater permits should qualify for over-the-counter permitting, with an inspection within 48 hours of installation scheduled through MyBuildingPermit.com. Kirkland's page does not mention an emergency exception, so ask Kirkland before starting work first.

Sources: City of Bellevue work requiring permits, plumbing permits and mechanical permits pages. City of Kirkland water heater replacement permit page and 2023 fee schedule.

What Stays The Same Either Way

Bellevue enforces the 2021 Uniform Plumbing Code through its city code, and other Eastside cities enforce their own adopted codes. Safety items such as venting, gas line sizing, seismic bracing and an expansion tank on a closed system matter in every city. The permit paperwork differs, so confirm details with the city where the home sits.

Why This Matters For Homeowners Near The City Line

Some Eastside neighborhoods sit close enough to a jurisdictional boundary that a homeowner genuinely isn't sure which city's rules apply to their address. That's a fair question to ask before scheduling a replacement, since the wrong permit type can mean a failed inspection and a second trip through the process. We confirm the correct jurisdiction and permit type before starting any water heater job, whether the address is in Bellevue, Kirkland, or one of the other cities we serve.

None of this changes the actual mechanical work involved in a water heater replacement. It changes the paperwork trail that confirms the work was done correctly, and getting that trail right the first time avoids a delay that has nothing to do with the plumbing itself.

How This Plays Out For A Multi-City Landlord Or Property Manager

If you own or manage rental properties across both Bellevue and Kirkland, this permit difference is exactly the kind of detail that's easy to miss when you're handling maintenance across a portfolio. A process that worked cleanly on a Bellevue unit doesn't automatically transfer to a Kirkland one. Assuming it does is a common way projects get delayed by a failed inspection tied to the wrong permit type, not any actual problem with the installation.

We track jurisdiction-specific requirements like this as a standard part of any multi-property relationship. It's the kind of administrative detail that's easy to get right once you know it, but easy to get wrong if you assume every Eastside city works the same way.

How To Confirm Which Rules Apply To Your Specific Address

The most reliable way to confirm which permit process applies to a given address is to check directly with the city or county the property is legally within, rather than assuming based on the mailing address or general neighborhood. City boundaries don't always align neatly with how an area is commonly referred to, and a property with a Kirkland mailing address can, in some cases, actually sit in unincorporated King County or vice versa.

We verify jurisdiction as a standard first step before starting any permitted plumbing work. Getting this wrong doesn't just create paperwork friction. It can mean work needs to be redone or re-inspected under the correct process, which costs real time and money to fix after the fact.

What This Looks Like For An Out-Of-State Contractor Or DIY Buyer

If you're new to the area, whether as a homeowner tackling your own repair or hiring based on experience from a different state, it's worth knowing that permit requirements genuinely vary city to city here in a way that isn't universal everywhere. Some regions have more uniform county-wide permitting. The Eastside's patchwork of independent city permit departments alongside King County's own process means the same job can involve different paperwork depending on the exact address.

This isn't unique to water heaters specifically, the same jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction variation applies to plenty of other permitted plumbing work. It's a good general habit to confirm the specific city's requirements before assuming based on experience from elsewhere, or from a neighboring Eastside city that happens to work differently.
