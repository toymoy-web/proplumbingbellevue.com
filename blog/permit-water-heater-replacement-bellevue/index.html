Bellevue Water Heater Replacement Permit: What You Need

What actually applies to a Bellevue homeowner replacing a water heater, based on the city's own permit pages.

By the Bellevue Pro Plumbing team · September 2026 · Updated October 2026

If you are planning a water heater replacement in Bellevue, the City of Bellevue requires a permit. The permit comes from Bellevue's own development services, not from King County. Knowing which permit applies, and when you can start work, avoids a failed inspection and an awkward surprise when you sell.

What Triggers The Permit Requirement

Bellevue's permit page says a permit is required for replacing or moving an existing hot water heater. That applies whether you hire a licensed plumber or do the work yourself. Minor repairs such as clearing a clog or fixing a leaky faucet generally stay permit-free.

The line gets crossed when you add new plumbing, relocate a fixture, extend piping or alter piping inside a wall, ceiling or beneath a floor. A water heater replacement is on the permit side of that line even when the new unit goes in the same spot.

Water Heater Permits In Bellevue

Bellevue issues its own plumbing and mechanical permits. A permit is required for replacing or moving an existing water heater. Replacing an electric water heater needs a plumbing permit, and replacing a gas water heater needs a mechanical permit.

The city does not require a permit before an emergency repair, but you must obtain the permit on the first city work day after the work started. Permits that do not require plan review can be issued the same day, and inspections still follow.

Sources: City of Bellevue work requiring permits, plumbing permits and mechanical permits pages, WAC 51-56-0500.

Gas Versus Electric: Which Permit

The city's plumbing permit page lists replacing an electric water heater as work that needs a plumbing permit. It says replacing a gas water heater requires a mechanical permit instead. Boilers and any water heater over 199 MBH are permitted by the state Department of Labor and Industries rather than the city.

A conversion between fuel types, such as gas to electric, can involve more than one permit because gas lines, venting and electrical circuits are all affected. We confirm the full list of permits before starting any conversion.

What About An Emergency?

Bellevue has a practical rule for failures that cannot wait. The city says it does not require a permit before getting an emergency repaired, but you must obtain a permit for that work on the first City of Bellevue work day after the work started. It lists replacing broken hot water heaters among the common examples.

If your tank is flooding a closet on a Saturday night, you do not have to wait for city offices to open. The permit still has to be pulled promptly on the next work day. When we handle the job, we take care of that step as part of the emergency call.

How Fast Is The Permit

The city says plumbing permits that do not require plan review can be issued the same day, and mechanical permits that do not require plan review can also be issued the same day. For a standard replacement that usually means the permit is not what slows the project down.

The permits still require inspections. Schedule the inspection promptly after installation, and leave the water heater accessible so the inspector can see the connections.

Seismic Bracing Applies Either Way

Separate from the permit rule, Washington's plumbing code requires water heaters to be anchored or strapped to resist movement in an earthquake. The state code calls for straps in the upper third and the lower third of the tank, with the lower strap at least four inches from the controls.

If your existing tank has no bracing, or the straps have loosened or corroded, fix that during the replacement. It is a quick job compared with everything else involved.

Can A Homeowner Pull Their Own Permit?

Bellevue's pages we reviewed do not describe a homeowner exemption in a way we can confirm, so do not assume one applies. Ask the city's permit staff what they require from a homeowner applicant. If you hire a licensed plumber, we handle the permit and the inspection scheduling as part of the job.

What To Expect On Installation Day

A typical replacement starts with shutting off the water and power or gas, draining the old tank and disconnecting it. The new unit is set, connected, vented as the fuel type requires and strapped. We then fill the tank, check for leaks and test the controls before we leave.

Ask your plumber to confirm the relief valve discharge, the shutoff valves and any expansion tank your system needs. These details matter for safety and are worth reviewing before the inspector arrives.

Repair Or Replace First

Not every failing water heater needs a new tank. A bad thermostat, heating element, igniter or relief valve can often be repaired, and a repair does not trigger a replacement permit. A leak from the tank body is different. A tank that has corroded through cannot be repaired, and it can flood a closet quickly.

If your unit is leaking from the tank, shut off the cold water supply and the power or gas, then call. We will tell you whether a repair makes sense or whether replacement is the better choice, and we will explain the permit steps before any work starts.

Age, repair history and the cost of the repair all matter in that decision. A short conversation about your options before you commit is worth the time, especially if you are planning to sell or rent the home soon.

If this sounds like what you're dealing with, our water heater installation and replacement page covers what a visit involves, and water heater repair is worth a look if the unit may still be fixable.
