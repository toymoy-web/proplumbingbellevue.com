Not every plumbing problem fits neatly into one category. This is where the calls that don't land on a specific service page end up: a running toilet that won't stop, a pipe leak in an unusual spot, a fixture failing in a way that's hard to describe over the phone. We diagnose these the same way we'd diagnose anything else, in person, before quoting a fix.

A lot of these calls turn out to be smaller than the homeowner feared; a running toilet is usually just a worn flapper, not a bigger problem. But we've also found things that started as a small complaint and turned out worth catching early, like a slow pipe leak that hadn't yet caused visible damage. Every repair is completed to Uniform Plumbing Code standards regardless of how minor the original call seemed.

A fair number of these calls come from homeowners who tried a DIY fix first, a running toilet flapper swapped from a home improvement store, a supply line hand-tightened, and the problem either didn't fully resolve or came back within days. We're glad to pick up from wherever a DIY attempt left off rather than starting over, and we'll tell you honestly if the original fix was actually on the right track.

We also get a fair number of calls ahead of a home sale or purchase, where a buyer's inspection flagged a plumbing item that needs addressing before closing, or a seller wants issues resolved proactively to avoid a negotiation delay. These calls tend to be time-sensitive in a way that's different from a routine repair, since a closing date doesn't move the way a homeowner's own schedule might, and we prioritize accordingly when a repair is genuinely tied to a real estate deadline.
