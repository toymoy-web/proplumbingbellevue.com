TestingAnnual

Backflow preventers are required on properties with irrigation systems, fire suppression systems, or certain commercial connections, and most local water utilities require annual testing to confirm the device is still functioning. We perform the test, certify the results, and file with your utility where required.

If a test finds the device isn't holding, we repair or replace it on the spot in most cases, since a failed backflow preventer is a compliance issue that utilities generally want resolved quickly. Installation and testing follow the Uniform Plumbing Code's backflow prevention requirements, the standard Washington State has adopted.

Most of our backflow calls come from property managers and homeowners who received a notice rather than from a visible problem, and the notice itself usually comes from the water utility serving that specific address. Bellevue draws water through several utility service areas depending on the neighborhood, so we confirm which utility applies to a given property before scheduling, since the exact filing requirement can differ slightly by provider.

Beyond irrigation systems, we also test backflow preventers on fire suppression lines, which some commercial and multi-family Bellevue properties are required to maintain separately from any irrigation-related device.

These fire-line preventers are inspected on the same annual cycle but often fall under a different reporting requirement, and property managers sometimes assume one test covers both when it doesn't.

We confirm which devices are actually on a property before scheduling, so nothing gets missed on the paperwork side even if the physical testing itself is straightforward.

Why Your Water Utility Is Asking About This

This service usually comes up because a homeowner or business received a notice from their water provider requiring annual backflow testing, not because of a visible plumbing problem. A backflow preventer stops contaminated water from being siphoned back into the clean water supply, and utilities require it be tested yearly on properties with irrigation systems, fire sprinklers, or certain commercial fixtures.

Bellevue Rules

How Bellevue Handles Backflow Test Reports

Bellevue Utilities says state law requires backflow assemblies to be tested annually by a certified backflow assembly tester. The city's cross connection control program expects testers to keep current certification and test kit calibration on file with the city.

The customer is responsible for getting the report submitted on time, and the program says non-cooperation can lead to termination of water service. We test the device and give you the paperwork, so the report is ready to send.
