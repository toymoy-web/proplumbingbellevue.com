A water heater's service life runs about 10 to 12 years, and past that point, a repair is often a stopgap rather than a real fix. We replace failing tank units and, increasingly, install tankless systems for homeowners who want continuous hot water and a smaller footprint, common in Bellevue's newer condos and townhomes where mechanical space is limited.

Sizing matters more than most homeowners expect. A tank that's too small for the household runs out of hot water during back-to-back showers. A tankless unit that's undersized for the home's fixture count struggles during simultaneous use. We size the replacement to the actual household, not a one-size default.

For older Bellevue homes still running a standard 40- or 50-gallon tank, we also check the surrounding plumbing during replacement. A tank replacement is the easiest time to catch a failing shutoff valve or a missing expansion tank, both common findings on original 1970s-era systems.

For homeowners who've never gone through a water heater replacement before, the process is more straightforward than it sounds: we remove the old unit, install and connect the new one, and test it under normal household use before we leave. The parts of the process that take longer, permitting for a tankless conversion, or a code-required expansion tank on an older system, are things we flag during the initial assessment, not as a surprise mid-job.

We also handle water heater replacements tied to a home sale, either a buyer's inspection flagging an aging unit or a seller replacing one proactively before listing. These tend to come with the same closing-date time pressure as other pre-sale plumbing work, and we prioritize scheduling accordingly. We can also provide documentation of the new unit's installation for the transaction.

Local Neighborhoods

Water Heaters From Downtown Condos To Lake Hills Ramblers

Water heater installs vary by neighborhood. In Lake Hills, Crossroads, and Newport Hills, ramblers and split-levels from the 1950s and 1960s usually keep a tank in a garage or utility closet, and a replacement is often a straightforward swap. Downtown, BelRed, and Factoria condos more often use tankless or compact units in tight mechanical closets, so venting, access, and building rules shape the job.

Pricing

What Affects The Price

Replacement cost depends mainly on tank versus tankless, the unit's capacity, and whether the install needs any code-required upgrades like an expansion tank or updated venting.

Tank vs. tankless

Tankless units cost more upfront but take less space and don't run out of hot water.

Capacity

A larger household needs a bigger tank or a higher-flow tankless unit, which changes the price.

Code upgrades

Some replacements require an expansion tank, updated venting, or a drain pan that the old install didn't have.

Access

A unit in a tight closet or attic takes longer to remove and replace than one in an open garage or basement.

Emergency Replacement

Replacing A Failed Water Heater Quickly

A tank that has failed and is flooding a closet cannot wait for a scheduled install. Our emergency line is answered around the clock, and we replace the unit when repair is not worth it.

Bellevue requires a permit to replace or move a water heater. A gas unit needs a mechanical permit and an electric unit needs a plumbing permit. The city allows emergency work to start first, with the permit obtained on the first city work day after the work begins.
